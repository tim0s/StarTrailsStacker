Questions and answers

What do I need?

A Mac with macOS 15 or later, and a series of photos of the night sky taken from a tripod, so the camera doesn’t move between photos.

Which cameras and file types work?

Star Trail Stacker reads RAW files from most cameras, using the RAW support built into macOS, as well as TIFF and JPEG files. If your camera’s RAW files can be opened in Preview or Photos on your Mac, they should work in Star Trail Stacker.

How should I take the photos?

Put the camera on a tripod, focus on the stars, and take photos one after another with an interval timer, ideally with short gaps between them. For example, the night on this website was shot with 25-second exposures every 40 seconds, for 1 hour and 47 minutes: 161 photos.

The longer you shoot, the longer the trails. Removing planes and satellites needs at least 5 photos.

Some plane or satellite trails are still visible. What can I do?

In the Clean Frames step, switch the trail search from Fast to Thorough. Thorough compares every photo at full resolution: it takes longer, but it also catches short streaks and faint dots.

The landscape was detected wrongly in a spot.

In the Foreground & Sky step, paint over the spot with the Landscape or Sky brush. Your corrections are kept, even if you change the look in Develop later.

Why does the app need free disk space?

While it works, Star Trail Stacker keeps the cleaned photos and other intermediate files, so it never has to redo a finished step. The Frames step shows how much space a night needs. You can choose where these files go, and delete them, in Settings › Intermediate Files.

Can I stop and continue later?

Yes. Finished work is kept when you pause or quit. Save a project (File › Save Project) to come back to a night later, with all your settings and mask corrections.

Are my photos uploaded anywhere?

No. Everything happens on your Mac, and the app doesn’t connect to the internet. See the privacy policy.
